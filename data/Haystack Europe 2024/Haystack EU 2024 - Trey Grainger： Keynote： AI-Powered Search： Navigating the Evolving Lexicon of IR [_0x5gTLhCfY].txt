What are bi-encoders, and how is this different from dense vector search like we've been doing for years and years? Bi-encoders are the idea of taking queries and documents and mapping them to the same vector space so you can use queries to search for documents. Typically with the bi-encoder, you either have separate encoders for the query and documents trained together, or you have one that's been trained to handle both. The idea is simply generating embeddings. Then, you take your query vectors and document vectors, perform a similarity of some sort, like a dot product or whatever, and generate some results. That's the way we do vector search.

The question is: what are cross-encoders, and how do they differ from learning to rank? Bi-encoders over here on the left—this is similar to the last slide you saw—have the idea of taking documents, passing them to the encoder, generating embeddings, and then indexing those embeddings, putting them in your search engine or vector database. Once you've done that, at query time, you take your query and encode it independently. Then you perform your vector similarity against all the vectors in the index. Maybe you're using some approximate nearest neighbor technique to make it more efficient, but the idea is you can independently encode both the query and the document, and then generate your similarity score. In general, this is efficient as vector comparisons go.

A cross-encoder is different. Remember earlier, we talked about learning to rank, and the idea behind learning to rank is building a ranking classifier. The ranking classifier uses judgments and what have you, and has a model, a generalizable model, to score and rank all results. A cross-encoder takes documents and a query in, but instead of encoding them separately, it encodes them together. The idea here is that each document and query are encoded together, which means you can use the context, the attention of your documents, to influence the interpretation of your query, and use the context of your query to influence the interpretation of your documents. What that means is that if I type a query for "mountain hike," the embedding generated for all documents is based on how those documents are related to "mountain hike." The context of "mountain hike" is added into their embedding. Generally speaking, you're going to get better relevance with a cross-encoder than with a bi-encoder because the documents can look across to the queries and the queries can look across to the documents and understand their context to generate better ranking about how they're related.

The downside of cross-encoders is that they're really slow. You can't take millions of documents and run them through a cross-encoder. Often you can't even take thousands; typically, you're going to take maybe the first page or two, or depending on how beefy your server is, the top results, and rerank those with a cross-encoder to get the best first, second, or third page. Keep in mind, this is encoding every query and every document pairwise together, which is slow, takes time, and you're not going to be able to wait for the search engine to do all that processing.
