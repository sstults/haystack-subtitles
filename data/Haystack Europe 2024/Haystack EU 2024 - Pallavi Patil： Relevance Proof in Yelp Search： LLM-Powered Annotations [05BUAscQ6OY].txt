To start, what is Yelp? This is a screenshot of one of Yelp's pages on the web. In one sentence, Yelp connects people with great local businesses. Businesses can jump onto Yelp and have their own pages where they provide information about themselves—what's on their menu, what services they offer, what their hours are, what kinds of payments they accept. Users, when they visit these businesses or use their services, can write reviews for them. So, what Yelp Search does is connect users with businesses they’re searching for. You might be looking for a new restaurant or have issues at home and need a plumber. You type your search and Yelp displays relevant businesses. 

To set some context, I'm sure most of you know this term already, but I'm going to be saying 'SERP' a lot. SERP stands for Search Engine Results Page. When you run a search, businesses are retrieved and ranked based on the query you’ve run and displayed on the SERP. For example, if you search for 'Korean corn dogs', you might see results that look something like an image page or listings specific to your intent. Now, annotations play a role here. Imagine if you ran a search and all you saw on the page were these results without additional information. Annotations provide additional context and relevance proof that the business meets the user’s query. Annotations could be of various types, providing relevance proof or adding unique business information, such as a personal appeal like 'happy hour' shown because you are known to a Yelp user who likes happy hours.

One specific type of annotation is 'review highlights'. These are spans or sentences of text from business reviews shown on search results to give extra context. Often they're bolded with relevant words or phrases like 'Pizza', proving that the search result matches the user's query. So, what makes a good annotation? Yelp has dual responsibilities: We need to accurately portray businesses and provide users with accurate, decision-making information. So we prioritize how annotations confirm a business’s relevance to the search query, highlight something unique, or offer supplementary necessary information, all baked into our annotator service. This sets priorities and principles to choose which business annotations to show.

Having revisited our previous example of Korean corn dogs, you see examples of annotations on these businesses fulfilling our product principles. We began annotations with fixed, structured kinds, leveraging business data. Over the last few years, we've aimed to build intelligence into our annotations by using more community-generated content and showing awareness of user intent or query context. We focus on different vision aspects and continue iterating on these features, leading us to discuss LLM-powered annotations. 

Query understanding and LLM expansions allow us to determine which annotations are relevant, needing to grasp what users want first. Users on Yelp have various intents like searching for specific business categories, dishes, services, or broader and more specific inquiries. Query highlight expansions create phrase sets to highlight applicable phrases on search results aligning with the user’s intent. An intuitive example is how expansion would work for 'Starbucks' by simply seeing highlights of related terms or categories—phrases that help in displaying value like 'affordable' in an 'affordable hair salons' search, where synonyms help in emphasizing the search result relevance.

We developed LLM expansions to expand queries into related phrases or key concepts. Initially starting with simple expansions and iterating to focus on reusability, we reached a modular expansion system applicable to various use cases. The system helps order key concepts or phrases into tiers of relevance, allowing differing use cases to cherry-pick desired tiers. For more details, there's a discussion available in a Haystack talk earlier this year detailing our evolution.

Developing intelligent annotations involved incorporating LLM expansions into existing infrastructures. Our original infrastructure matched highlights manually. We saw that incorporating expansions could assist better coverage, diversity, and quality in matched highlights. Such benefits provided more relevance, increased business coverage, and resolved quality issues by matching relevant phrases rather than just exact search queries. By expanding query understanding and allowing better control and filtering more candidates, including inappropriate content, stemming, and alignment at sentence boundaries, we evolved into a smarter retrieval flow.

In an architectural improvement, returning more top matches allowed better incorporation of user search intentions and grammatical coherence. Introducing a system of selectivity enriched annotations—smart retrievals that leverage understanding alongside features premium in our new ranking module. Textual data had unwanted formatting and required synthesis into a data set of relevant phrases to harness in annotations. They derive more value for lower content websites and provide critical relations through the data stored when matched against LLM expansions.

There are more ways to apply this to future annotations—integrating existing signals, using LLMs in selection directly, and devising new annotation opportunities from their evaluations. Strategies ensure flexibility between manual methods and LLM-enhanced methods; a balance we continually iterate upon due to the vastness and scope of the project demands.
