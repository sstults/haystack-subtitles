Vector search differs from keyword search—instead of table lookups, it traverses a graph. We're working with Lucene's HNSW index, and we wanted to discern what parameters affect speed and efficiency.

Our test situation involves 2.6 million indexed documents on a large machine with 128GB RAM to fit the entire index, and 100 concurrent users representing a typical busy day scenario on one of our major products. Each query was a unique vector to avoid cache testing, varying across four different models. Initially, tests showed poor performance because of unmerged segments causing inefficiencies. After merging segments, search latencies improved about tenfold.

Going forward, all tests are on fully optimized indexes. We've also checked how vector indexing impacts index size, shown in our first graph, with abstracts vectorized, significantly expanding the index size. Query side latencies were surprisingly good, with low delays across tested conditions.

Testing concurrent queries on multiple collections instead of one showed robust results, even with 50 million documents and 300 users. However, the data set size must fit in memory to avoid performance spikes.

We explored K-values, which determine the number of results and also relevance. Higher K-values can align top search results better but increase computation demands, as shown in the results with varying K-values. 

Exploring similarity functions—Euclidean, Cosine, and Dot Product—revealed Dot Product as the simplest and most performant under low K-values. However, Euclidean’s efficiency varied beyond 3,000 K, switching relative performance.

Finally, vector encoding uses float and scalar quantization to byte format, expected to reduce index size and potentially enhance speed. Our results confirmed index size reduction with significant gains in query speed, albeit with precision loss trade-offs, emphasizing the speed-cost-relevance balance.

Stress testing with increased user loads reaffirmed system stability under expected demand, backed by quantified results. This stress test underscored the importance of fitting indexes in memory.

Filtering uniquely affects Vector Search as filtering nodes alter graph traversal paths, raising latency unlike inverted index searches, where it lowers it. Our findings showed acceptable ranges for small models, but with more extensive filtering, the graph's traversability suffers until it switches over to brute force search.
